**Framework 1 — CLOSER (Gap Opening)**
The CLOSER framework: Clarify why they're there, Label the problem, Overview their past tries, Sell the vacation not the flight, Explain the plan, Reinforce the decision. In discovery, Clarify is the opening move — ask open questions to have the prospect articulate the problem in their own words before you say anything about LeadGrow.

Hormozi original: "What made you reach out today?" — let silence sit until they fill it. Do not rescue them with your elevator pitch.
B2B equivalent: Open with "What's going on in your pipeline that made this call worth your time?" then hold silence. Let the founder own the framing.
Anti-pattern: Rep fills silence with a product pitch. "So what we do is..." within the first 60 seconds kills diagnostic trust.
Coaching prompt: Did the rep open with a Clarify question and hold silence? Did the prospect articulate the problem unprompted, or did the rep hand them the framing?

**Framework 2 — Gap Framework (Qualification)**
Hormozi's Gap: identify the current state, identify the dream state, quantify the cost of inaction. In B2B discovery, this maps to BANT qualification — but the gap between now and the goal IS the sale.

Hormozi original: "Where are you now? Where do you want to be? What's it costing you every month you're not there?" (three sequential questions, don't rush)
B2B equivalent: "What's your pipeline looking like now — qualified meetings per week? ... What does good look like 6 months from now? ... What's a closed deal worth to you, and how many are you leaving on the table each month?" Surface the dollar gap before presenting anything.
Anti-pattern: Skipping the gap quantification and jumping to features. "We do 50 touches per prospect" before the prospect knows what they're losing.
Coaching prompt: Did the rep establish current state, dream state, AND cost of inaction? Was the gap quantified in the prospect's own currency (revenue, meetings, time) before any solution framing?

**Framework 3 — Peel the Onion (Pain Surfacing)**
Ask-dig-confirm to get to root-cause pain, not surface symptoms. Prospects describe symptoms first. The real pain is always one or two layers deeper.

Hormozi original: Prospect says "we need more leads." Rep asks "Tell me more — is it the volume of leads or the quality?" Prospect: "Quality, we get leads but they don't convert." Rep: "What does that mean for your team — are they burning time on unqualified calls?" Dig until the emotional + financial cost is visible.
B2B equivalent: Founder says "outbound isn't working." Rep asks "When you say not working — is it zero responses, or responses that don't convert to meetings?" Keep drilling: response rate → meeting rate → close rate → revenue. Find where the leak is and what it costs per month.
Anti-pattern: Accepting the first-level answer. Rep hears "we need more leads" and pivots to "we generate leads, here's how." Symptom accepted, root cause never surfaced.
Coaching prompt: Did the rep ask at least 2-3 follow-up questions to get below the first answer? Was the root-cause pain (not the symptom) stated explicitly by the prospect before the rep offered any framing?

**Framework 4 — Listen 2:1 (Talk Ratio)**
Hormozi rule: prospect talks twice as much as the rep. A rep who talks more than 33% of the call is pitching, not diagnosing. Listening IS the competitive advantage in discovery — most reps can't do it.

Hormozi original: "Your job in discovery is to be the world's best detective. You're gathering evidence, not presenting. Every question you ask is intel. Shut up and let them give you the close."
B2B equivalent: In a 30-minute discovery call, the rep should be talking for 10 minutes max. That means 3-4 well-placed questions, listening completely, then one tight reflection before moving on. Not 15 minutes of pitch tucked into "questions."
Anti-pattern: The filler question — rep asks a question, then while the prospect is mid-answer, is already framing the next pitch point. Result: prospect feels unheard, rep misses the real signal.
Coaching prompt: Estimate talk ratio from the transcript. Did the prospect speak more than the rep? When the prospect gave a long answer, did the rep reflect it back or immediately pivot to a new point?

**Framework 5 — Pain is the Pitch (Outcome Connection)**
Hormozi: the prospect's stated pain IS the pitch. You don't need slides. You reflect their pain back to them and connect it to the outcome LeadGrow delivers. If you've done discovery right, closing is just confirming what they already said.

Hormozi original: "So it sounds like the real problem is you're doing $500k a year but you know you should be at $1.5M, and the gap is a predictable pipeline. Did I get that right?" Then: "That's exactly what we solve for."
B2B equivalent: "So if I'm hearing you right — you're getting maybe 2 qualified meetings a month from outbound, each deal is worth $40k ARR, and you know you're leaving 3-4 deals on the table every quarter. That's $120k you're not closing because pipeline is unpredictable. Is that the real problem?" Then connect: "That's the gap we close for our clients — predictable qualified pipeline, not just activity."
Anti-pattern: Moving to solution without reflecting the pain back. Rep heard the pain, then jumped straight to "our process has 3 steps." The prospect never heard their own situation mirrored back — trust and urgency don't land.
Coaching prompt: Did the rep explicitly reflect the prospect's pain back using the prospect's own numbers/language before presenting any solution? Did the prospect confirm the pain framing before the rep moved on?
