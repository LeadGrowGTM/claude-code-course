This file guides an LLM extracting content from call transcripts. It must be prescriptive enough that an LLM following it produces usable output, not generic summaries.

**File structure:**

```
# Content Ideas Reference

This vector always runs — regardless of call type (sales, internal, general). Use this reference to extract 3-5 quotable moments and 3-5 content angles from every transcript.

## What This Vector Produces

Output format (include both sections in every report):
```
### Content Ideas

**Quotable Moments (3-5)**
1. "[Direct quote from transcript]" — [why this lands: the insight, the tension, the specificity]
2. ...

**Content Angles (3-5)**
1. [Angle title] — [1-2 sentence brief: the hook, the claim, the format it suits]
2. ...
```

## Quotable Moment Guidelines

A quotable moment is a verbatim or near-verbatim excerpt from the transcript that could stand alone as a LinkedIn post, a Twitter thread opener, or a newsletter pull quote.

**What qualifies:**
- A contrarian claim stated with conviction ("Most agencies optimize for activity. We optimize for pipeline. They're not the same.")
- A specific observation that collapses a common assumption (not "personalization matters" but "we saw 3x reply rates when we stopped writing emails that sound like emails")
- A real number or result attached to a specific action ("We got them to 11 meetings in month 2 — the first month was terrible")
- A moment where the speaker names a problem most people feel but haven't articulated
- A sharp analogy or reframe that makes something complex suddenly obvious

**What disqualifies:**
- Generic truisms ("relationships are important in sales")
- Incomplete context (the quote only makes sense if you were on the call)
- Jargon-heavy statements that need translation to be understood
- Filler ("yeah, totally, that's a great point")

**Extraction rule:** Pull the exact words from the transcript. Do not paraphrase. If it needs editing to land, note what the edit would be in brackets — but surface the raw quote first.

## Content Angle Guidelines

A content angle is a structured idea for a piece of content (post, thread, newsletter section, short video) that this call makes possible. It is NOT a summary of what was said — it is an argument or insight the transcript supports.

**What qualifies:**
- A claim the call proves or illustrates (even if the call was just one data point — note that)
- A process or framework that emerged from the conversation that others would want to steal
- A tension or tradeoff the conversation revealed that most people aren't talking about
- A "here's what I learned from a call this week" format where the lesson generalizes

**Angle format (for each angle):**
```
[Angle title] — [The core claim or hook] | Format: [post / thread / newsletter / video] | Hook: [the opening line that would make someone stop scrolling]
```

**Examples of good angle extraction:**
- Call reveals a founder burned $30k on a previous agency with zero accountability. Angle: "Why agency accountability is a structural problem, not a people problem" | Format: LinkedIn post | Hook: "The founder told me they had no idea what happened to their $30k. Turns out, they were never supposed to."
- Call surfaces that the prospect's reps are doing manual prospecting 3 hours/day. Angle: "The hidden cost of manual prospecting no one tracks" | Format: thread | Hook: "Your reps aren't slow. They're just doing $15/hour work with $150/hour people."

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- Angles that require the reader to have been on the call to understand
- Angles that are just summaries ("we talked about outbound strategy today")
- Overfitting to this specific prospect (make it generalizable)

## Output Count

Always extract exactly 3-5 quotable moments and exactly 3-5 content angles. If the call is thin on content (short, purely logistical), note that and extract what's available — do not pad with weak entries.

Output format:

### Internal Team Output

**Meeting Summary**

- ...

- ...

- [Blocker description] — blocking: [what it's blocking] | owner: [who resolves it] | status: open
- ...
```

## Decisions Guidelines

A decision is a commitment the team made on the call — a choice between options, a direction set, a policy established. It is NOT a discussion point or an idea floated.

**What qualifies as a decision:**
- "We're going to [X] instead of [Y]"
- "The cutoff is [date/metric]"
- "Going forward, [policy]"
- A clear "we agreed" or "let's do" moment

**What does NOT qualify:**
- "We should think about X"
- "It might be worth exploring Y"
- A question raised but not resolved

**Format rule:** Every decision must have an owner (the person accountable for it) and a deadline if one was mentioned. If no owner was stated, flag it: "owner: UNASSIGNED — needs owner before next meeting."

## Action Items Guidelines

Action items are specific, completable tasks assigned to a named person. The LLM must assign an owner for every action item — if the transcript doesn't name one, flag it explicitly.

**Hard rule: no orphaned action items.** Every action item in the output must have:
1. A named owner (person on the call, or flagged as UNASSIGNED)
2. A due date or "TBD" (not left blank)
3. A context note explaining what it unblocks or why it matters

**What qualifies:**
- Explicit assignment ("Mitchell, can you send that over by Friday?")
- Implied assignment (only one person on the call could own it — name them)
- Self-assigned ("I'll handle that")

**What does NOT qualify:**
- "Someone should look into X" — flag as UNASSIGNED, surface as a blocker
- Post-meeting tasks that weren't explicitly mentioned but are obviously needed — do not invent tasks not discussed

**Extraction rule:** Use the person's first name as stated on the call. If unclear, use the email domain or participant label from the transcript header.

## Blockers Guidelines

A blocker is anything that prevents a decision from being executed or an action item from completing. Surface it explicitly — do not bury it in the summary.

**Blocker format:**
```
- [What the blocker is] — blocking: [what it prevents] | owner: [who resolves it] | status: open
```

**Resolved blockers:** If the call resolved a previously open blocker, note it as: `status: resolved on this call`.

**What qualifies as a blocker:**
- A dependency on an external party (waiting on [Name] / waiting on legal / waiting on a vendor)
- A missing decision that's preventing action
- A resource constraint named on the call (bandwidth, budget, access)

**What does NOT qualify:**
- Risks or uncertainties (those go in the summary)
- Items that are in progress and not stuck

## Output Quality Gates

Before finalizing the internal team section:
1. Every action item has a named owner or explicit UNASSIGNED flag
2. Every decision has an owner
3. No open blocker is buried in action items or summary — it must appear in the blockers section
4. The meeting summary does not repeat the decisions verbatim — it provides context and outcome
