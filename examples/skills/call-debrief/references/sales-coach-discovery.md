# Sales Coach — Discovery Call Reference

Loaded by the Sales Coach vector when `call_type = sales/discovery`.
Score each dimension X/10. Produce an overall score at the end.

---

### Gap Opening

**Hormozi (original):** The CLOSER framework's Clarify step — "Before I tell you anything about what we do, can I ask you a few questions?" Opening with permission. The gap question creates space: "What made you take this call today?" or "What's going on in your business that made you want to look at outbound?" Let them say the problem. If you fill the gap yourself, you've closed it.

**B2B equivalent:** Opening with "What's prompting you to look at outbound right now?" instead of pitching. Creating space for the founder/VP to articulate their pipeline problem in their own words. The rep waits through silence — doesn't jump to fill it.

**Anti-pattern:** Opening with "Let me tell you about LeadGrow..." or "We typically work with companies like yours..." — the rep fills the gap before the prospect has spoken, eliminating the prospect's ownership of the problem.

**Coaching prompt:** "Did the rep open with a question that created space, or did they pitch first? Did the prospect articulate their pipeline problem in their own words before the rep described the service?"

### Pain Surfacing

**Hormozi (original):** Pain is the Pitch. "What's that costing you?" / "How long has that been going on?" / "What have you tried?" The goal is the root-cause pain, not surface symptoms. First answer is always the polished one. Real pain is three layers down. Keep peeling: "And what happens because of that?" The pitch is not the product — the pitch is the articulated pain.

**B2B equivalent:** After prospect says "our pipeline is inconsistent," not accepting that. Peeling: "What does inconsistent mean — no meetings at all, or meetings that aren't converting?" → "How long has it been inconsistent?" → "What have you tried to fix it?" → "What's it costing you in terms of missed revenue or runway pressure?" The coaching is about reaching founder-level pain (growth pressure, investor pressure, CAC anxiety) not surface symptoms.

**Anti-pattern:** Accepting the first answer and moving to the solution. "We hear that a lot — here's what we do." Surface-level pain = commodity conversation. Root-cause pain = premium conversation.

**Coaching prompt:** "Did the rep ask at least 2–3 follow-up questions after the prospect's first pain statement? Did they reach a financially or strategically material pain point, or did they stay at symptom level?"

### Outcome Connection

**Hormozi (original):** After surfacing pain, connect it explicitly to the outcome they want. "So if I understand correctly, the real issue is [root cause], and what you actually want is [outcome]?" Make them confirm. The sale is made when the prospect hears their own pain reflected back precisely. The product is only introduced as the bridge between their pain and their desired outcome.

**B2B equivalent:** "So the core issue is that you're not generating enough qualified pipeline from outbound, which is putting pressure on your Q3 targets — and what you actually want is a consistent flow of right-fit meetings without having to manage the infrastructure yourself. Is that right?" Then: "That's exactly the problem we exist to solve." Not "here are our features."

**Anti-pattern:** Pitching the service without connecting it to their specific stated pain. Generic outcome language: "We help companies grow their pipeline." That's not an outcome connection — it's a brochure.

**Coaching prompt:** "Did the rep explicitly reflect the prospect's pain back to them before describing the service? Did the prospect confirm the pain summary? Was the service introduced as the bridge to their stated outcome?"

### Qualification

**Hormozi (original):** You are not selling to everyone. Qualification is respect for your own time and the prospect's. "Do you have budget set aside for something like this?" / "Are you the person who'd make this call, or would others be involved?" / "How soon are you looking to move on this?" Unqualified prospects waste everyone's time. Better to disqualify fast than to chase for months.

**B2B equivalent:** Budget: "Do you have budget allocated for outbound, or would this require a new budget conversation?" Authority: "Are you the decision-maker here, or would your co-founder / head of sales need to be involved?" Need: already established in pain surfacing. Timing: "Are you looking to move on this in the next 30–60 days, or is this more exploratory?" If timing is vague, the deal isn't real yet.

**Anti-pattern:** Skipping qualification because the conversation feels warm. Running a full demo or proposal for someone who can't buy. Assuming authority because the prospect has a VP title.

**Coaching prompt:** "Did the rep confirm budget, authority, and timing before moving to next steps? Were any of the three left unaddressed? If so, which one — and does the rep know?"

### Talk Ratio

**Hormozi (original):** The rule is simple: the prospect should talk twice as much as you. If you're talking more than 33% of the time on a discovery call, you are pitching — not discovering. Silence is your friend. Let them fill it. Every minute they talk is a minute they're selling themselves. The rep's job is to ask questions and listen, not to demonstrate knowledge.

**B2B equivalent:** On a 30-minute discovery call, the rep should talk for approximately 10 minutes (questions + brief context). The prospect should speak for 20 minutes. If the rep is explaining the service for more than 5 minutes total, the ratio is wrong.

**Anti-pattern:** Talking through the full pitch because the prospect was "engaged." Mistaking questions with long answers for permission to keep talking. Over-explaining the three pillars before the prospect has confirmed they have the problem.

**Coaching prompt:** "Estimate the talk ratio from this transcript. Was the prospect speaking more than the rep? If the rep talked more than 40% of the time, flag it — what was being said in that time?"
